PaletteML

Color relationships, learned from real paintings.

Pick a color and get companion colors recommended by a model trained on dominant colors extracted from museum artwork — not a color-wheel rule, not a language model.

Choose a color

Add a color and click “Generate Palette” to see recommendations.

How this works

  1. Dominant colors are extracted from real paintings by clustering each image's pixels in CIELAB space — a color space designed so distance between colors tracks human-perceived difference.
  2. Every extracted color is mapped onto a shared color vocabulary — a fixed set of representative colors found with K-Means over thousands of dominant colors pooled across the training paintings.
  3. PPMI (positive pointwise mutual information) measures which vocabulary colors tend to appear together in the same painting, more often than chance — the raw signal for "these colors go together."
  4. SVD factorizes that co-occurrence structure into a lower-dimensional embedding, so colors with similar relationships to the rest of the vocabulary end up close together — even pairs that never directly co-occurred in the training set.
  5. Recommendations are ranked by cosine similarity in that learned embedding space, seeded by whichever color(s) you choose above.

Evaluation

The model was evaluated with a held-out, leave-one-color-out test against two baselines: a popularity baseline (always recommend the most common colors) and a random baseline. Of the approaches tested, the SVD embedding produced the strongest results and improved on the direct PPMI/co-occurrence model — though that improvement was not statistically significant at this evaluation's sample size. Both learned models clearly outperformed random. Full methodology and numbers are in the repository's evaluation report, linked below.
